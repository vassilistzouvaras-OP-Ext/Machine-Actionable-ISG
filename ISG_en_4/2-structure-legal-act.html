2. Structure of a legal act

Legal acts follow a strict structure and – depending on the complexity of the text – parts, titles, chapters or sections may be used in the preamble, enacting terms and annexes.

This diagram shows the basic elements of a legal act. Depending on the complexity of the text, elements such as parts, titles, chapters or sections may be used in the preamble, enacting terms and annexes.

  • 2.1. Title
    The complete title of an act comprises the type of act, the number, the name of the author, the date of adoption and the subject matter.
  • 2.2. Preamble
    Preamble means everything between the title and the enacting terms of the act.
  • 2.3. Articles (enacting terms)
    The enacting terms, which constitute the normative part of the act, are divided into articles.
  • 2.4. Compulsory character of regulations
    A formula defining the compulsory character of regulations, with a special typographic presentation, follows the final article.
  • 2.5. Concluding formulas (place, date and signature)
    The act ends with the words ‘Done at …, …’ indicating the place and date of the signature, followed by the signature(s).
  • 2.6. Annexes
    The annex to an act generally contains rules or technical data that, for practical reasons, do not appear in the enacting terms.
