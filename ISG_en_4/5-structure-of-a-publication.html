5. Structure of a publication

The structure of a publication comprises various parts such as the cover, the title page, edition elements (e.g. copyright notices) and presentation elements such as references and quotations.

The structure of a publication comprises various parts such as the cover, the title page, edition elements (e.g. copyright notices) and presentation elements such as references and quotations.

  • 5.1. Cover
    The cover of a book consists of four pages referred to as the front cover, the inside front cover, the inside back cover and the back cover.
  • 5.2. Title page
    The title page features the editor, the title of the work, the number of the edition and/or the year, the seat of the editor, the author’s name and, if applicable, the volume number or the title of the collection.
  • 5.3. Verso of the title page
    The verso of the title page has distinctive components.
  • 5.4. Copyright
    Copyright protects original literary, artistic and scientific works from the moment they are created.
  • 5.5. Preliminary pages and end matter
    The preliminary pages (also called the front matter) include the dedication, foreword, preface and introduction, while the index is part of the end matter.
  • 5.6. Divisions of the text
    The text of a work must be presented in such a way that a reader finds it easy to follow, which can be done by breaking it down into divisions and subdivisions and using different numbering systems.
  • 5.7. Listed points
    Listed points can be basic lists, with four basic types, or multiple listed points, which follow the rules for the division of text using numbers, letters, dashes and bullets.
  • 5.8. Emphasis
    Bold type – often used in headings and titles – can be used in running text to show changes of subject, to highlight keywords or for emphasis in the same way that some other languages use italics, but should be used sparingly.
  • 5.9. References
    A reference is a precise way of locating a quotation, or citing a work or a part of a work relevant to the subject in question, either in the text or in a footnote.
  • 5.10. Quotations
    A quotation is made up of quoted passages from other works or of words and thoughts of third parties reported in the text.
  • 5.11. Artistic material (illustrations)
    Photographs, drawings, diagrams and graphs are used to illustrate a publication.
  • 5.12. Tables
    Tables serve to illustrate or explain the text in more detail.
