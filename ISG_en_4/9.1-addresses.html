9.1. Addresses

In principle, the international format is always used in the works of the European Union institutions; the language to use depends on whether the work is unilingual or multilingual.

In principle, the international format is always used in the works of the European Union institutions; the language to use depends on whether the work is unilingual or multilingual.

  • 9.1.1. Addresses: general principles
    In principle, the international format is always used in the works of the European Union institutions.
  • 9.1.2. Addresses in unilingual documents
    For unilingual texts, different rules and recommendations apply for mail to countries that use the Roman alphabet, for non-EU countries that do not use the Roman alphabet and for Member States that use the Cyrillic or Greek alphabet.
  • 9.1.3. Addresses in multilingual documents
    For multilingual texts, lists of addresses can be composed once only so that they are harmonised and consistent.
  • 9.1.4. Addresses in the Member States: presentation and examples
    An example of a multilingual list, included here, shows how addresses in the Member States are presented.
  • 9.1.5. Addresses in the Member States: specific characteristics
    The postal codes of the Member States vary – presence of country codes, spaces or hyphens – and the language to use for the addresses in a work depends on various factors.
