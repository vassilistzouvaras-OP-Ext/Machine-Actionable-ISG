10.1. Punctuation

Punctuation marks (full stop, question mark, exclamation mark, colon, semicolon, etc.) help to separate the elements of a text and make its meaning understandable.

Punctuation marks (full stop, question mark, exclamation mark, colon, semicolon, etc.) help to separate the elements of a text and make its meaning understandable.

  • 10.1.1. Full stop
    A full stop marks the end of a sentence, a footnote, truncations at the end of a word and run-in side heads.
  • 10.1.2. Question mark
    Every question which expects a separate answer should be followed by a question mark.
  • 10.1.3. Exclamation mark
    An exclamation mark is used after an exclamatory word, phrase or sentence.
  • 10.1.4. Colon
    A colon is most often used to indicate that an expansion, qualification, quotation or explanation is about to follow.
  • 10.1.5. Semicolon
    Use a semicolon rather than a comma to combine two sentences into one without a linking conjunction or to separate items in a long or complex series.
  • 10.1.6. Comma
    A comma separates items in a series, linked sentences connected by a conjunction, parenthetical and introductory phrases and non-defining relative clauses.
  • 10.1.7. Brackets
    Brackets can be round and used like commas to add a non-essential insertion into the text, or square and used to make editorial insertions in quotations.
  • 10.1.8. Dashes and hyphens
    Dashes may replace commas or brackets, while hyphens are used for compound nouns and adjectives, for prefixes, related or contrasting pairs, and to replace ‘to’ in a number or date range.
  • 10.1.9. Quotation marks
    Quotation marks should be curly (‘…’) rather than straight ('…').
  • 10.1.10. Ellipsis
    An ellipsis is three points indicating an omission in the text.
  • 10.1.11. Forward slash
    The forward slash may be used to mean ‘per’ and in fractions, to denote a 12-month period that does not coincide with a calendar year and to give alternatives.
  • 10.1.12. Apostrophe
    Apostrophes mark the possessive form, contractions or the plurals of single lower-case letters.
