# Representative Questions for `ISG_en_4web.pdf`

These questions are designed to be representative of the scope and level of the *Interinstitutional Style Guide* and cover its major parts: the Official Journal, general publications, conventions common to all languages, and English-specific rules.

## General Scope and Purpose

1. What is the main purpose of the *Interinstitutional Style Guide* within the institutions, bodies, offices and agencies of the European Union?
2. How does the guide distinguish between rules for the *Official Journal* and rules for general publications?
3. Why does the guide place strong emphasis on harmonisation across multiple official languages?
4. Which entities are responsible for governing, coordinating and updating the style guide?
5. Why does the guide state that its uniform conventions prevail over previously used or externally proposed solutions?

## Part One: Official Journal

6. How did the title and linguistic coverage of the *Official Journal* change between 1952 and 2007?
7. What was the significance of the creation of the `L` and `C` series in 1968?
8. What role did the `S` series and the TED database play in the publication system of the European Communities and later the European Union?
9. Why was the renaming to *Official Journal of the European Union* in 2003 important?
10. How did the Treaty of Lisbon affect the classification of acts published in the *Official Journal*?

## Structure of the Official Journal

11. What is the difference between the `L` series and the `C` series of the *Official Journal*?
12. What kinds of material are typically published in the `L` series?
13. What kinds of material are typically published in the `C` series?
14. Why is the numbering of acts and documents an important editorial issue in the *Official Journal*?
15. What information should a properly prepared contents page for an issue of the *Official Journal* provide?

## Structure of a Legal Act

16. What are the main structural components of a legal act according to the guide?
17. What is the function of the title of a legal act, and what must it identify clearly?
18. How do citations differ from recitals in the preamble of a legal act?
19. What is the role of the articles, or enacting terms, in a legal act?
20. Why are annexes and subdivisions important for the clarity and usability of legal acts?

## Drafting and Referencing Rules

21. How should references to the *Official Journal* be presented in EU drafting?
22. What is the difference between referring to an act by its full title and referring to it in shortened form?
23. Why does the guide include specific rules for references to subdivisions and amendments of acts?
24. Why does the order of citation matter when listing treaties, authors, countries, languages or currencies?
25. What are the main presentation issues to consider when drafting lists in legal or administrative texts?
26. Why does the guide include rules for defining an expression or a word inside a legal text?
27. What kinds of editorial problems can arise if international agreements are not cited consistently?

## Part Two: General Publications

28. What responsibilities are divided among authors, authorising departments, the Publications Office and printshops?
29. Why does the guide stress the importance of a logical structure in original documents or manuscripts?
30. What is the difference between a monograph and a continuing resource?
31. Why are identifiers such as ISBN, ISSN, DOI and catalogue numbers necessary in EU publications?
32. What is the function of a title page and the verso of the title page in an official publication?
33. Why does the guide include detailed rules about copyright notices, reuse authorisation and disclaimers?
34. What is the purpose of preliminary pages, bibliographies and indexes in a publication?
35. Why are references, quotations, illustrations and tables treated as separate editorial topics?

## Part Three: Conventions Common to All Languages

36. Why does the guide provide common typographic and revision conventions for all language versions?
37. What is the purpose of proof-correction marks and word-processor spacing rules in multilingual publishing?
38. Why does the guide standardise the way countries, languages and currencies are designated and abbreviated?
39. What practical problems can occur if footnotes are not ordered and referenced consistently?
40. Why are addresses, electronic addresses and telephone numbers treated as style issues rather than merely factual data?
41. Why is the official listing order of EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies important?
42. What is the value of standardising the official titles of Commission directorates-general and services?

## Part Four: Publications in English

43. Why does the guide provide English-specific house rules in addition to the common interinstitutional conventions?
44. According to the guide, when should a full stop not be used?
45. What is the difference between the use of a question mark in a direct question and in indirect speech?
46. Why does the guide discourage the use of exclamation marks in formal texts?
47. When should a colon be used, and when should it not be used in headings or before tables and figures?
48. In what situations does the guide recommend a semicolon rather than a comma?
49. What is the significance of the distinction between defining and non-defining relative clauses for comma use?
50. How does the guide distinguish between the functions of round brackets and square brackets?
51. Why does the guide advise using dashes sparingly, especially in legislation?
52. What principles govern the use of hyphens in compounds, prefixes and ranges?
53. Why does the guide prefer curly quotation marks and a restrained use of quotation marks in formal English texts?
54. How should ellipses be spaced and punctuated in formal English-language publications?
55. Why do topics such as upper and lower case, numbers, dates, time, italics, abbreviations and symbols require explicit house rules?
56. Why is gender-neutral language included as a dedicated topic in the English-language part of the guide?

## Annexes and Reference Material

57. Why are annexes on emblems, abbreviations, acronyms, country codes, currency codes and language codes useful for editors and drafters?
58. How can the multilingual lists in the annexes support consistency across institutions and languages?
59. Why is an index particularly important in a style guide of more than 300 pages?

## Application Questions

60. If you were preparing a new EU publication in English, which parts of the guide would you consult first, and why?
61. If a text contains inconsistent references to legislation, currencies and language versions, which chapters of the guide would be most relevant to fixing it?
62. How would you use the guide differently when editing a legal act for the *Official Journal* versus a general information brochure?
63. If a multilingual publication contains inconsistent institution names and country codes, which sections of the guide would help you standardise them?
64. Why is this guide not only a style manual but also a tool for legal, administrative and interinstitutional consistency?
